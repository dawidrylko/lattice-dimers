# Glossary

The words this repository uses for its pieces, lattices, surfaces, signs, site and code, in the README files, the notes, the site, the research notes, the papers and the code comments. Use these words and avoid the ones listed against them. A new term, or a correction to one, is added here in the same change that introduces it.

Three exceptions. The OEIS entry texts in [`oeis/`](../oeis) are quoted as they stand on the OEIS, so they keep the OEIS wording ("diagonal domicule", the link text of a `%H` line). An avoided name is avoided as the name of one notebook, page or tab; a section that describes two surfaces together may name both. Text that a script prints into a recorded output keeps its wording until the output is recomputed, so [`research/king-entropy/src/cw.py`](../research/king-entropy/src/cw.py) still prints "king lattice" into `out/out_cw.txt`.

## Lattices

A lattice says which cells of a board are neighbours, and so which pieces a tiling may use. The code picks one with its `lattice` option, `square`, `triangular` or `king` (`sq`, `tri` or `king` in [`research/king-entropy/src/tm.py`](../research/king-entropy/src/tm.py)).

| term | meaning | where it is used | avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| square lattice | the graph whose vertices are the cells and whose edges join cells sharing a side; on a rectangle it is the grid graph, and its perfect matchings are the domino tilings | the cylinder and torus notebooks of the three menu groups, the names of the benchmark tables Dominoes on cylinders and Dominoes on tori, the cylinder and torus solvers ([`js/README.md`](../js/README.md)), the controls of the research scripts | |
| triangular lattice | the square lattice with one diagonal in every square face, the one that runs down to the right (↘), so a tiling uses dominoes and ↘ diagonal pieces; the project counts it for comparison | the Pieces switches of the gallery and the benchmarks (Triangular lattice), the Triangular lattice group of the site menu and its notebooks ([`notes/triangular-lattice/`](../notes/triangular-lattice)), the comparison of three lattices in the Domicules ▸ Tori notebook, the rectangle, cylinder and torus solvers of all three lattices, the controls of the papers and the research scripts | "Triangular" alone as the label of the menu group |
| king's graph | the square lattice with both diagonals, so every cell is joined to its eight neighbours; its perfect matchings are the domicule tilings | the titles of both papers, the notebooks, the site, the research notes and scripts | "king graph" and "king-graph", "king lattice", also in a list next to the square and triangular lattices |

## Surfaces

A board is the h × w grid of cells a tiling covers, on any surface; the gallery's Board picker and the code use the word that way. A surface says which edges of the board are glued. The Surface switches of the gallery and the benchmarks pick one, so they name the surface in the singular; a notebook or a benchmark table covers many boards of one surface, so it names them in the plural.

| term | meaning | where it is used | avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rectangle | a board with no glued edges; the 2n × 2n board is the square case | the Surface switches of the gallery and the benchmarks (Rectangle), the gallery sentence of the README and the site, the screen-reader names of the gallery sheets and the notebook drawings ("Domino tiling number 3 of a 4 by 4 rectangle") | "board" for the rectangle alone, as in "a board, cylinder or torus" |
| cylinder, cylinders | a board of circumference w and height h whose left and right edges are glued and which is open at the top and the bottom | the Surface switches of the gallery and the benchmarks (Cylinder), the names of the benchmark tables Dominoes on cylinders, Triangular lattice on cylinders and Domicules on cylinders, the Cylinders item of each menu group (`notes/<group>/02-cylinders.md`, page `notes/<group>/cylinders.html`) | "Tori and cylinders" as the name of one notebook or tab |
| torus, tori | a board whose left and right edges and top and bottom edges are both glued; tori is the plural, as in the literature | the Surface switches of the gallery and the benchmarks (Torus), the names of the benchmark tables Dominoes on tori, Triangular lattice on tori and Domicules on tori, the Tori item of each menu group (`notes/<group>/03-tori.md`, page `notes/<group>/tori.html`) | "Torus" as the label of a notebook in the menu or the name of its file or page, except the page `torus.html` that only redirects |

| seam | the loop along which a pair of glued edges meet, so a cylinder has one seam and a torus two; a piece that joins the last and the first column crosses the column seam, and on a torus a piece that joins the last and the first row crosses the row seam | the cylinder and torus notebooks, the cylinder and torus solvers and the renderer, the research notes | |

## Signs

A signed count weighs each tiling with a sign instead of counting it once. This repository uses two. The seam sign comes from the column seam, and the word twisted marks it in the code and the research notes. The crossing sign comes from the unit squares in which two diagonal pieces cross. No flag sets it and no solver prints it.

| term | meaning | where it is used | avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| seam sign | the sign (-1)^k of a tiling of a cylinder or a torus, where k is the number of pieces that cross the column seam; the row seam of a torus is not counted. The signed count is the difference between the two parities of those crossings, and the papers call the sign a Z2 flux | the Domicules ▸ Tori notebook (section The seam sign), the cylinder and torus solvers, the research notes and the papers | |
| crossing sign | the sign (-1)^k of a domicule tiling of a rectangle, where k is the number of unit squares in which two diagonal pieces cross; it counts no pieces across a seam, so it is not the seam sign. Wildeboer and Seidel give an orientation of the king's graph under which the Pfaffian is plus or minus the sum of the tilings with this sign. The tests take the orientation in which a cell points at the cell below it and the cell to its right with -1 in an even row and 1 in an odd row, and at the two cells diagonally below it with 1; on each of their ten boards its Pfaffian is plus or minus that sum. On the boards 3 × 3, 4 × 4, 5 × 4, 6 × 6 and 7 × 8 that orientation is the mirror image, with the rows in reverse order, of the one in Fig. 1(b) of their paper (arXiv:1711.10717), continued periodically, with the arrows at some cells reversed, which changes the Pfaffian by its sign at most. No solver prints the sum. The note on the crossing polynomial also takes the sign on cylinders and tori, where a unit square across a seam counts like any other, and proves that the sum is never negative on rectangles and on cylinders and tori whose periods are even and at least 4 | the kernel of `domicule-rectangle-modular` in the three languages and the test of each language that compares the sum with that Pfaffian, the introduction of the king entropy paper ("pairs of crossing diagonal dimers") | "crossing sign" for the sign of the pieces that cross the column seam, which is the seam sign; the field `crossing` of a move in the cylinder and torus solvers means that seam |
| crossing polynomial | the polynomial Z_H(t), the sum of t^x(T) over the tilings T of an induced subgraph H of the king's graph, where x(T) counts the unit squares in which two diagonal pieces cross; at t = 1 it counts the tilings and at t = -1 it is the sum with the crossing sign. On a cylinder or a torus a unit square across a seam counts like any other | the note on the crossing polynomial ([`research/crossing-polynomial/README.md`](../research/crossing-polynomial/README.md)) | |
| crossing-free | said of a domicule tiling in which no two diagonal pieces cross | the note on the crossing polynomial | |
| designated square | a unit square of a tiling whose straight pair, chosen by the checkerboard rule (both horizontal pieces when the row and column of its top left cell add up to an even number, both vertical pieces when odd), lies in the tiling | the note on the crossing polynomial | |
| twisted | counted with the seam sign | the option `twisted` and the flag `--twisted` of the cylinder and torus solvers, the field `twisted` of the test cases in `spec/cases/`, the twisted transfer and its twisted levels in the research notes | "twisted" in the prose of the notebooks and the site, where the name is seam sign |
| twisted cut, twisted swap | a cut where two rows agree up to the left-right reflection J, and the swap of the parts below it; neither has anything to do with the seam sign | the notes on log-convexity, `log-convexity/involution.py`, the companion note | |

## Site

The site has the gallery and the benchmarks on its main page and the notebooks on their own pages. The header menu groups the notebooks by the kind of piece or lattice, and each group lists them by surface. The groups of the menu and the buttons of both Pieces switches go from the square lattice through the triangular lattice to the king's graph: Dominoes, Triangular lattice, Domicules. Each lattice adds a diagonal to every square face of the one before it, the triangular lattice the one that runs down to the right and the king's graph the other, so the tilings of a board on each lattice include those on the one before it.
